Assumptions and sensitivity

What Phase 1 delivers

Rooms and keys

Two Signature Suites inside the mansion, holding seven bedrooms between them, and five premium two-bedroom cabins in the garden. Twelve rooms, let as seven keys.

Everything around them

The Dome as a destination dining pavilion, a commercial kitchen, the garden pool and sun terrace, and Kids' World: the Barn club, adventure playground, shaded kids' pool and animal welfare upgrades.

The seventh bedroom

The extra bedroom sits inside an existing suite, so it adds sleeping capacity rather than a new key. Its fit-out is absorbed within the mansion interiors budget and the contingency, and the forecast on page 2 is unchanged.

The one lever it opens: a four-bedroom suite can carry a higher weekend rate than a three-bedroom one. That rate is a commercial decision for Namou's principals, and the forecast will be reissued once it is set.

Assumptions

Rates and revenue

Signature Suite AED 8,000 at weekends and 6,000 midweek; cabin AED 4,000 and 2,000. Blended for weekend-weighted family demand, rising 3 per cent a year from 2029.

Activities are bundled into the rate, with premium sessions, day visitors, destination dining at the Dome and off-peak events charged separately. Food and beverage is modelled at half of room revenue.

Profit is operating profit before depreciation, and no rent is payable under the joint-venture structure.

Sensitivity

If rates settle 30% below plan

Stabilised revenue is around AED 8.9M with operating profit around AED 2.9M, and payback extends to the fifth operating year.

What cushions the downside

The Dome's food and beverage, the day programme and events carry revenue that does not depend on room rates.

Common to every route

The estate enters the venture through a fifty-year registered musataha. The existing hotel licence enables nightly letting and licensed food and beverage. No rent is paid to any shareholder, and all returns flow through the venture.
